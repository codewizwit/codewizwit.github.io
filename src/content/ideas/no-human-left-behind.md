## What it means

Two things are true at once, and neither one cancels the other.

I love AI and use it constantly. It changed the way I think, build, and
create, and I want other people to have that. I am also aware of the damage it
can do, to jobs, to creativity, to human connection. That awareness has not
resolved into comfort and I do not pretend it has.

The tension is the engine. Everything that keeps me up at night about AI gets
built into frameworks, tools, and ways of working that keep humans at the center
of adoption. The position I take is the harder middle: using the thing you
are worried about, responsibly, while helping other people do the same. Hold
both truths and build anyway.

**"Everyone" is a specific list.** The cynics. The scared. The
junior devs. The senior engineers who feel threatened. The non-technical people
who have been told they cannot build. Adoption that works for the early adopters
and stops there is adoption that failed.

Underneath it sits a claim about software itself. Technology is never neutral.
Every technical decision shapes who thrives, because software redistributes
power, influences culture, and determines access. AI is not inevitable. In my
words from the 2025 manifesto, it is choices wearing a lab coat.

## Why it matters now

The AI conversation is measured in tokens processed, models trained, products
delivered. Those metrics tell you how fast you are moving. They do not tell you
whether you are sprinting off a cliff.

While the numbers go up, inequities deepen, decision-making concentrates in
fewer hands, and systems amplify the biases technology was supposed to solve.
The manifesto names this a values problem, and
one that keeps reaching production.

The timing argument is harder than the ethics argument. The defaults
organizations are setting right now, about who gets to build, who gets to
decide, and who gets handed a finished tool, will hold for a decade. That is the
argument in [Don't standardize the human out](/ideas/three-layers), and it is the
reason this is urgent rather than aspirational.

## What it looks like in practice

It works as a filter. Five questions decide whether a project, a role, or a
decision passes:

1. Does this expand opportunity or concentrate it?
2. Who benefits, and who might be harmed?
3. Does this keep humans in meaningful control?
4. Is the human impact considered alongside the technical outcome?
5. Could I explain this to someone affected by it?

Everything else I have built answers to those. The Lift is how a whole team
gets there without losing its slower adopters. Shift Left moves thinking and
quality to the front of the work. Encoding Intent puts human judgment in before
the model writes anything. Capturing What Worked encodes judgment that proved
itself, before it fades. [The Four Lenses](/ideas/four-lenses) check whether any of
it is serving the people involved.

Empathy is the operational skill underneath all of it, and **testing is how
empathy scales**. Testing asks what could go wrong for the person using this.
Quality is care made visible in the code. Automation gives that care
consistency. You cannot claim to care about users without verifying the system
works for them.

## The objection, and the answer

**"This is values talk. Where is the engineering?"**

The commitment already has a mechanism, and the mechanism is encoding.

An encoded lesson does not check who is reading it. Judgment that took one
person years to develop becomes the floor everyone starts on. A rule written
into the environment reaches the junior developer and the principal engineer at
the same moment, with no difference in access and no meeting required. That is
what turns a stated value into a system.

Individual speed resets every sprint. A class of problem that stopped arriving
never comes back.
